Conclusion

In conclusion, Latin influenced the syntax of modern programming languages to a large extent. Although words originating from English’s Germanic roots may account for a greater overall usage within syntax, the concentration of Latinates within mathematical, administrative and metaphorical categories show that Latin has had a significant breadth of influence on the conceptual vocabularies used to describe complex ideas within the field of Computer Science, both within code and the systems and platforms that they make up. Latin’s role as the ‘language of learning’ is solidified by its pervasiveness within technical and specialised words, even in a sphere as modern and ever-developing as computing.

The dominance of English-based syntax, documentation and resources relating to programming does indeed pose challenges to EAL programmers, specifically in the initial understanding of keywords and concepts drawn from English words, but this does not hinder them from making meaningful contributions to the field. In fact, they are the fastest growing demographic of contributors to the open-source projects we use today.

With the introduction of localised syntax, increasing development of programming resources in different languages, and basing programming aptitude on the ability to problem solve and partake in computational thinking rather than simple mastery of ever-changing syntax, we can take into account the vast cultural and linguistic backgrounds of many programmers - and therefore make Computer Science a more accessible field for everyone.
