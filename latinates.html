Programing Concept Latinates

Many math - and subsequently Computer Science terminology can be traced back to Latin.

Since Computer Science is a rapidly developing field - verba parienda sunt - new words have to be created. Words to describe new concepts are usually ‘created’ by using prefixes and suffixes (which are commonly traced back to Latin as well!)

Latinates are words directly derived from or influenced by Latin. Some notable conceptional Computer Science Latinates are detailed below:

Computing, from Latin ‘computare’, meaning to count or calculate

Algorithm from the Latin "algorismus" (Latinised form of the name Al-Khwarismi.) Mohammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi was a mathematician that made significant contributions to algebra and is also linked to the introduction of the decimal numbers in Europe!

Operating system: From Latin "operatus" past participle (verb form) of working/producing and Greek "systema", meaning something that is organized into a whole, made of individual parts.

Program: Latin’s "programma", made of the prefix “pro-” (meaning before) + Greek "grámma" which means letter. Together, meaning "something written beforehand"

Cursor: Latin “cursum” (a runner) as a cursor runs across a screen

Portal - Latin "porta", meaning gate or entrance

It’s clear that Latinates are present in Computer Science concepts and names, but what about within programming language syntax?
