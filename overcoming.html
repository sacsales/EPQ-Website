Overcoming Barriers

Within programming resources, avoiding slang and keeping examples culturally agnostic allows students from wider cultural backgrounds to follow the curriculum without the added layer of complexity of an unfamiliar context.

Glossaries, or dedicated lists of programming keywords (especially those very rarely encountered in real life, or those with different everyday meanings) that can be easily revisited throughout a coding curriculum, not just during the beginning stages - can serve as a useful reference for EAL programmers throughout their learning journey.

The use of visuals and graphics that show control flow or relationships between objects can illustrate more complex concepts without the need for more complex keywords.

Inline dictionaries within browsers and localised code ‘blocks’ for beginners (in block-coding languages such as Scratch and Hopscotch) can take away the initial roadblock of unfamiliar keywords and focus on developing computational thinking and logic. Gradually adding keywords in as programmers progress can reduce cognitive load and make the transition to english-based code easier.
