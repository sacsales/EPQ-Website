Processing Results

From this data, I took the 50 most frequent words in each corpus and traced their etymology using 2 websites: the Oxford English Dictionary (OED), and the Etymonline website (since some etymology entries were locked behind a paywall on the OED website.)

My tokeniser ignored single character words (such as ‘a’ or ‘i’). If a word was a compound or abbreviation I traced the more general form or meaning of the word within the context of programming. For example, ‘db’ is often a contraction of ‘database’, or ‘int’ was looked up as ‘integer’.

Classifying Unique Words

Just like words in English are classified by usage (adjectives, nouns, verbs etc.), words within project source code can be divided into categories.

Source code files contain 3 things:

  • Regular programming syntax, as seen in the documentation. This makes up the majority of code.
  • Identifiers, which are names for variables and functions (There are specific conventions one is meant to follow to capitalise them, like camelCase or PascalCase, but the content is decided by the programmer themself)
  • Comments (annotations within code that act as self-documentation, which helps when debugging/collaborating with others)

Across these categories, I decided to further divide them into ‘classifications’ based on their use in programming syntax and relative meanings:

  • Grammar: Words usually appearing within code comments and therefore used in grammatically correct sentences, although some are also used in regular code! Words in this category span articles, prepositions, conjunctions and such. Examples: the, is, to, of, this

  • Mathematical: Words relating to the domain of maths. Examples: integer, function, value, expression

  • Logical: Words relating to logical expressions. Examples: and, or, if, not

  • Administrative: Words relating to file-keeping, organisation, and management. Examples: reference, database, parse

  • Metaphor: Many words used in programming were borrowed from physical objects due to similarity in function or behavior. Examples: stack, string, key, state

  • Communication: Words where the original purpose was to communicate information between people. Examples: define, name, print, assert

Programming vocabulary is not derived primarily from mathematics. Instead, much of it comes from ordinary human communication. English grammar, and physical metaphors are used to make abstract computational concepts understandable!
